Connectivity in China has two layers: getting a data connection (easy) and what that connection can reach (the part people forget). Sort both before you land and the arrival is painless.

## Your three options

**eSIM (our default recommendation for short trips)**
Buy a China eSIM from a reputable international provider before you fly. It installs over Wi-Fi at home and connects the moment the plane doors open. Because your traffic routes outside mainland China, your usual apps — WhatsApp, Google Maps, Instagram — keep working normally. Typical cost: US$5–20 for 1–5 GB.

**Physical SIM at CKG airport (best value for long stays)**
Counters on the arrivals level sell tourist SIMs; bring your passport for registration. Expect roughly ¥50–100 for a month of generous data. You get a Chinese number — handy for hotel registrations and courier calls. Your traffic stays inside China, so blocked apps stay blocked without a roaming layer.

**Home-plan roaming (the zero-setup fallback)**
Works immediately, keeps your apps working like at home, and costs the most. Fine for a 72-hour visit; painful for a week.

## The firewall, honestly

If you use a Chinese SIM or local Wi-Fi without any routing trick, WhatsApp, Google services, Instagram and most Western news sites simply won't load. Travellers handle this three ways: foreign eSIM/roaming (simplest), a roaming layer on a Chinese SIM, or their own VPN arrangement installed before arrival. Choose at home, not at the airport.

## The app list we install for guests

| App | Why |
|---|---|
| **Amap (Gaode)** | The maps locals use; full English mode, better data than anything else in Chongqing's multi-level streets |
| **Alipay** | Payments + metro/bus codes (see our payments guide) |
| **WeChat** | Messaging + backup payment; some vendors only show WeChat codes |
| **Didi** | Ride-hailing with English UI and in-app payment |
| **Pleco or Apple Translate** | Offline Chinese dictionary; camera translation for menus |
| **Trip.com / 12306** | Train tickets — book high-speed rail with English interface |
| **AirVisual** | Chongqing fog is famous; air quality is usually fine but check before hikes |

## Practical arrival sequence

1. Land, connect to CKG free airport Wi-Fi.
2. Activate the eSIM (or buy the physical SIM at the counters).
3. Install/open Amap, verify it locates you, pin your hotel using the **Chinese address** from your booking.
4. Test Alipay with a ¥3 bottle of water at the arrivals convenience store. If that works, everything else will.
