Chongqing Jiangbei International Airport (CKG) is modern, well-signed in English, and — for a city of 30 million — remarkably manageable. What trips people up isn't the airport. It's the forty minutes after it. Here's the sequence that works.

## 1. Before you leave home

- **Install the apps while you still have home Wi-Fi:** Alipay, WeChat, Amap (Gaode Maps, has English), Didi, and a translation app with offline Chinese.
- **Link a card to Alipay or WeChat Pay.** As of late 2026 both accept most foreign Visa/Mastercard for payments inside China. Do the identity verification at home — the facial scan is much easier on your own sofa than on airport Wi-Fi.
- **Screenshot your hotel address in Chinese.** Not the English name — the Chinese address and phone number. You'll show this to taxi drivers and the check-in desk.

## 2. At the airport (30–60 minutes)

1. **Skip the currency exchange queues** unless you specifically want cash. Cards work at the airport; street-level Chongqing runs on phone payments.
2. **Get connected.** Two good options: an eSIM bought before flying (installs over Wi-Fi, works the moment you land — the practical choice for most travellers), or a physical SIM at the airport counters on arrivals level (bring your passport; plans are cheap, roughly ¥50–100 for a tourist month). Roaming also works fine in Chongqing — China's firewall applies by routing, so foreign-SIM roaming keeps your usual apps working.
3. **Buy a metro card or set up transport codes** in Alipay's "Transport" mini-app — Line 10 links the airport straight to the city.

## 3. Getting to your hotel

- **Metro Line 10** to Hongtudi, then transfer toward the city centre — cheapest, totally fine with luggage at off-peak hours, about ¥7.
- **Official taxi** from the rank outside the terminals: ¥60–100 to Jiefangbei depending on traffic. Only use the official queue — ignore anyone who approaches you inside the hall.
- **Didi** works well here; the app has an English interface and you pay in the app.

## 4. Check-in (the step everyone underestimates)

Chinese hotels must register foreign guests with the police, which means every field on the registration form matters and the desk will sometimes struggle with non-Chinese names. Have ready: passport, booking confirmation, and patience. If the desk asks for a Chinese phone number, your new SIM solves it. If the Wi-Fi login asks for a code sent by SMS — same.

## 5. Dinner without drama

Your first meal sets the tone. If you want hotpot, look for a busy place with locals queueing — a full dining room is the only review that matters. Point at what neighbours have, or use the translation app camera on the menu. Saying **bú yào là (不要辣, "no chilli")** is completely normal and no one will blink. Jiefangbei and Guanyinqiao both have late-night options well past 22:00.
