Let's kill the myth first: Chongqing food is not a chilli challenge with extra steps. Locals eat mild food constantly — breakfast is rarely spicy, kids eat here happily, and the city has a whole parallel cuisine of soups, dumplings and sweets. You just need to know where to look, because the loud red signage points at the fire, not the comfort.

## The phrase that fixes everything

**Bú yào là (不要辣)** — "no chilli". Add **wēi là (微辣)** if you want mild warmth. Write it on your phone, show it, and kitchens will comply without the slightest judgement. Every local has a relative who can't take heat.

## Ten dishes with zero chilli (that locals genuinely eat)

1. **Shāomài & clear-broth wontons (抄手)** — the breakfast of the city; ginger and vinegar, no heat
2. **Tomato-egg noodles (番茄鸡蛋面)** — sweet-sour broth, silky egg ribbon; xiaomian's gentle cousin
3. **Dòuhuā fàn (豆花饭)** — silky tofu over rice with mild savoury sauce; a lunchtime institution
4. **Steamed pork ribs with taro (粉蒸排骨, ask for no chilli version)** — soft, savoury, nourishing
5. **Jiācháng dòufu (家常豆腐)** — home-style tofu; specify bú yào là and it's pure comfort
6. **Sweet roasted sweet potatoes** — sold from street carts all winter
7. **Brown-sugar rice cakes (红糖糍粑)** — chewy, caramelised, dangerous to walk past
8. **Iced brown-sugar jelly (冰粉)** — the city's favourite summer dessert, chilli-free by definition
9. **Glutinous rice balls in sweet soup (小汤圆)** — warm, mild, perfect after a foggy walk
10. **Yogurt drinks (酸奶)** — every convenience store; the local antidote of choice

## Strategy: eat like a local kid

The chilli-heavy stuff dominates dinner menus aimed at groups. Breakfast and lunch are your mild windows — noodle shops, wonton houses and canteens serve gentle food all day. If you do go for hotpot, order a **split pot (鸳鸯锅, yuānyāng guō)**: half fiery, half tomato or mushroom broth, and nobody at the table compromises.

## Neighbourhoods that reward the gentle palate

- **Jiaotong Street (交通街) area** — old-school snack streets with wonton and tofu-rice counters where non-spicy is the default
- **Ciqikou ancient town** — touristy but useful: vendors understand "no chilli" in English and the sweet snacks are excellent
- **Guanyinqiao back lanes** — family-run dumpling shops where lunch costs ¥20 and nothing burns

## Vegetarian note

Genuine vegetarian (素, sù) food exists — Buddhist temple restaurants are fully vegetarian by tradition — but many "vegetable" dishes use pork fat or lard for flavour. Say **chī sù (吃素, "I eat vegetarian")** and kitchens adapt; for strict vegetarian or halal requirements, message us and we'll point you to the right kitchens personally.
